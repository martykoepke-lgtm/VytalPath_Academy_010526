# **VIDEO 2: PRACTICE MANAGEMENT vs EHR — UNDERSTANDING YOUR SYSTEMS**

**Video Type:** Core Lesson (Instructional) **Duration:** 5–6 minutes **Tone:** Clear, practical, conversational **Placement:** Module 1 lesson — could replace or supplement the reading lesson `pm-vs-ehr`

## **SCENE 1: The Two Systems in Every Clinic**

### **AVATAR SCRIPT —** Copy directly into HeyGen….do not speak \[pause\] or other words in brackets. have a nice relaxed speaking cadence. Before Avatar is shown create an intro image/slide that has the name of the section. A title intro or relevant B role that is ambulatory healthcare related.  Then transition to Avatar. \[warm\] Walk into almost any medical office in the country, \[pause\] and there are two software systems running behind the scenes. \[pause\] Every schedule, every patient record, every insurance claim, every message — \[pause\] it all flows through these two systems. \[pause\]

\[instructional\] The first is the \[emphasis\] Practice Management system, \[pause\] or PM. \[pause\] The second is the \[emphasis\] Electronic Health Record, \[pause\] or EHR. \[pause\]

\[matter-of-fact\] Some clinics have them as one integrated platform. \[pause\] Some have them as separate systems that talk to each other. \[pause\] But conceptually, they handle two different sides of the business. \[pause\]

### **PRODUCTION NOTES**

* Delivery: Set the scene. Every learner should feel like this is the missing context they've needed.  
* Tone: Grounding, practical. "This is how every clinic works."

---

## **SCENE 2: What the Practice Management System Does**

### **AVATAR SCRIPT — Copy directly into HeyGen**

\[instructional\] The PM system is the \[emphasis\] business engine of the practice. \[pause\] It handles everything related to running the practice as a business. \[pause\]

\[matter-of-fact\] Scheduling — \[pause\] this is where you build the day. \[pause\] You see open slots, book appointments, manage cancellations and reschedules. \[pause\]

Patient demographics — \[pause\] name, address, phone number, emergency contact, employer. \[pause\] This is where that information lives and gets updated. \[pause\]

Insurance information — \[pause\] the subscriber, the plan, the group number, the authorization. \[pause\] When you verify eligibility, you're working in the PM. \[pause\]

Billing and claims — \[pause\] once the provider finishes with a patient, charges flow into the PM, \[pause\] claims get built and submitted, payments get posted. \[pause\]

And reporting — \[pause\] daily reconciliation, accounts receivable, no-show tracking. \[pause\] The PM generates the business intelligence the practice needs to operate. \[pause\]

\[warm\] Bottom line: \[pause\] if it involves scheduling, money, or patient contact information — \[pause\] you're in the PM. \[pause\]

### **PRODUCTION NOTES**

* Delivery: Methodical walkthrough. Each function gets its own beat.  
* Tone: Like giving a tour of a building — "This is the scheduling wing, this is the billing department."

---

## **SCENE 3: What the EHR Does**

### **AVATAR SCRIPT — Copy directly into HeyGen**

\[instructional\] Now, the EHR. \[pause\] The EHR is the \[emphasis\] clinical engine. \[pause\] It handles everything related to the patient's medical care. \[pause\]

\[matter-of-fact\] Clinical documentation — \[pause\] when a provider examines a patient, they document their findings, assessment, and plan in the EHR. \[pause\] These are the clinical notes. \[pause\]

Orders — \[pause\] lab orders, imaging orders, referral orders. \[pause\] When a provider says "I need a CBC" or "Let's get an MRI" — \[pause\] that order is created and tracked in the EHR. \[pause\]

Results — \[pause\] when lab results come back, when imaging reports are finalized — \[pause\] they flow into the EHR and appear in the patient's record. \[pause\]

Medications — \[pause\] the medication list, allergies, and electronic prescriptions. \[pause\] When a provider sends a prescription to the pharmacy, that's the EHR. \[pause\]

And clinical messaging — \[pause\] message pools, task lists, nurse triage queues. \[pause\] When you route a patient's phone message to the nurse — \[pause\] you're putting that message into the EHR. \[pause\]

\[warm\] Bottom line: \[pause\] if it involves what happened medically — \[pause\] diagnoses, treatments, test results, prescriptions — \[pause\] that's the EHR. \[pause\]

### **PRODUCTION NOTES**

* Delivery: Mirror the PM walkthrough structure. Same pace, same rhythm.  
* Tone: Parallel construction reinforces the distinction between the two systems.

## **SCENE 4: How They Connect**

### **AVATAR SCRIPT — Copy directly into HeyGen**

\[instructional\] Now here's where it gets interesting. \[pause\] These two systems don't work in isolation. \[pause\] They pass information back and forth all day long. \[pause\]

\[matter-of-fact\] The bridge between them \[pause\] is the \[emphasis\] encounter. \[pause\]

When you schedule an appointment in the PM, \[pause\] the PM creates a scheduling container — \[pause\] a time slot, a patient, a provider, an encounter type. \[pause\]

When that patient arrives and is checked in, \[pause\] the encounter \[emphasis\] opens in the EHR. \[pause\] Now the clinical side can document. \[pause\]

When the provider finishes and selects their billing codes, \[pause\] those charges \[emphasis\] flow back to the PM \[pause\] where the claim is built and submitted. \[pause\]

\[instructional\] So the encounter starts in the PM, \[pause\] passes through the EHR, \[pause\] and comes back to the PM for billing. \[pause\] That's the cycle. \[pause\]

\[warm\] And as front desk, \[pause\] you're at both ends of that cycle. \[pause\] You start the encounter with scheduling and check-in, \[pause\] and you close it with check-out, follow-up, and billing support. \[pause\]

### **PRODUCTION NOTES**

* Delivery: This is the "aha" moment. Build to the encounter as the connecting concept.  
* Tone: Connecting the dots. Make the learner see the full picture.

# **VIDEO 3:ENCOUNTERS & ENCOUNTER SELECTION**

VytalPath Academy — Module 2 Opener

*REFINED SCRIPT — Under 5 Minutes | Corrected Encounter vs. Appointment Type Terminology*

**Video Type:** Core Lesson (Instructional)

**Duration:** 4–5 minutes

**Tone:** Practical, precise, stakes-aware

**Placement:** Module 2 opener — could supplement or replace reading lesson clinic-encounter-types

**Est. Word Count:** \~680 spoken words at \~150 WPM \= \~4.5 min with pauses

**SCENE 1: What Is an Encounter?**

Do not speak \[pause\] or other words in brackets. Have a nice relaxed speaking cadence. Before Avatar is shown create an intro image/slide that has the name of the section. A title intro or relevant B-roll that is ambulatory healthcare related. Then transition to Avatar. Do not add any brand logos. Maintain existing style preferences.

**AVATAR SCRIPT — Copy directly into HeyGen**

\[warm\] Every time a patient interacts with a healthcare organization — \[pause\] whether they walk into the clinic, call on the phone, or have lab work ordered — \[pause\] the system creates something called an \[emphasis\] encounter. \[pause\]

\[instructional\] An encounter is the system’s record of that specific interaction. \[pause\] It captures what happened, \[pause\] who was involved, \[pause\] what was billed, \[pause\] and what needs to happen next. \[pause\]

\[matter-of-fact\] A patient can have dozens — even hundreds — of encounters over their lifetime. \[pause\] Each one is a separate record. \[pause\] They’re all tied together by the patient’s \[emphasis\] Medical Record Number — their MRN. \[pause\] One patient, one MRN, forever. \[pause\]

\[instructional\] Each encounter gets its own \[emphasis\] Financial Identification Number — the FIN. \[pause\] One visit, one FIN. \[pause\] The MRN stays the same. \[pause\] The FIN changes with every encounter. \[pause\]

**Delivery:** *Foundational. Take time with MRN vs FIN — first time many learners will hear this.*

**Tone:** *Precise but not clinical. Make it stick.*

**SCENE 2: Clinic Encounters and Appointment Types**

**AVATAR SCRIPT — Copy directly into HeyGen**

\[instructional\] When you schedule an appointment, \[pause\] the system creates a \[emphasis\] clinic encounter. \[pause\] That’s the container — the record in the EHR for that visit. \[pause\]

\[matter-of-fact\] But you don’t just create the encounter and move on. \[pause\] You apply an \[emphasis\] appointment type \[pause\] to that clinic encounter. \[pause\] New Patient. \[pause\] Established Patient. \[pause\] Medicare Annual Wellness Visit. \[pause\] Each of those is an appointment type — \[pause\] and it gets attached to the clinic encounter you just created. \[pause\]

\[instructional\] That appointment type determines everything downstream. \[pause\] How much time the provider gets. \[pause\] What documentation they complete. \[pause\] Which billing code gets used — \[pause\] and how much the practice gets paid. \[pause\]

\[emphasis\] Wrong appointment type \[pause\] means wrong time, wrong documentation, wrong billing code. \[pause\] That’s a denied claim. \[pause\] The practice doesn’t get paid. \[pause\]

\[warm\] So when you select “New Patient” versus “Established Patient” — \[pause\] that’s a billing decision. \[pause\] A compliance decision. \[pause\] And it starts with you. \[pause\]

**Delivery:** *Build the stakes gradually. Don’t scare — inform.*

**Tone:** *"This is important and you can handle it."*

**SCENE 3: Common Appointment Types**

**AVATAR SCRIPT — Copy directly into HeyGen**

\[instructional\] Let’s walk through the appointment types you’ll use most. \[pause\]

\[matter-of-fact\] \[emphasis\] New Patient — NP. \[pause\] Someone who hasn’t been seen by this provider or specialty group in three years. \[pause\] Longer slots — 30 to 60 minutes. \[pause\] Full demographic entry, insurance setup, comprehensive intake. \[pause\]

\[matter-of-fact\] \[emphasis\] Established Patient — EST. \[pause\] Seen within the past three years. \[pause\] Shorter slots — 15 to 20 minutes. \[pause\] You verify and update rather than build from scratch. \[pause\]

\[matter-of-fact\] \[emphasis\] Medicare Annual Wellness Visit — AWV. \[pause\] This trips people up. \[pause\] It is \[emphasis\] not a physical exam. \[pause\] It’s a health risk assessment and prevention plan. \[pause\] Medicare covers it once per year. \[pause\] Schedule it as a regular physical and the claim gets denied. \[pause\] Make sure the appointment type and the patient’s expectations match. \[pause\]

\[matter-of-fact\] \[emphasis\] Transitional Care Management — TCM. \[pause\] Follow-up after a hospital or facility discharge. \[pause\] Seven days for complex, fourteen for moderate. \[pause\] Your job: schedule within that window and confirm the discharge date. \[pause\]

\[matter-of-fact\] Others you’ll see: \[pause\] \[emphasis\] Preventive visits — the annual physical. \[pause\] \[emphasis\] Procedure visits — injections, biopsies, minor surgeries. \[pause\] \[emphasis\] Consults — specialist referral visits. \[pause\] \[emphasis\] Pre-ops — surgical clearance. \[pause\] And \[emphasis\] urgent or same-day visits \[pause\] squeezed into held-open slots. \[pause\]

\[warm\] Every one of these is an appointment type \[pause\] applied to a clinic encounter when you schedule the visit. \[pause\]

**Delivery:** *Encyclopedic but efficient. NP, EST, AWV, TCM get depth; others are brisk.*

**Tone:** *Teacher mode. Name it, define it, show why it matters, move on.*

**SCENE 4: Beyond the Clinic**

**AVATAR SCRIPT — Copy directly into HeyGen**

\[instructional\] Not every encounter is a scheduled appointment. \[pause\] The system also tracks \[emphasis\] phone encounters \[pause\] for clinical calls, \[pause\] \[emphasis\] eRx encounters \[pause\] for prescription changes without a visit, \[pause\] \[emphasis\] lab-only and imaging-only encounters, \[pause\] and \[emphasis\] prior authorization encounters. \[pause\]

\[warm\] These are different encounter types — \[pause\] not appointment types on a clinic encounter. \[pause\] You won’t create all of them yourself, \[pause\] but you need to know they exist \[pause\] because patients, providers, and the system will reference them. \[pause\]

**Delivery:** *Broadening the picture in minimal time. Awareness, not mastery.*

**Tone:** *Efficient. Clear contrast with the clinic encounter model.*

**SCENE 5: The Front Desk Decision & Close**

**AVATAR SCRIPT — Copy directly into HeyGen**

\[warm\] So let’s bring this back to you. \[pause\]

\[instructional\] When a patient calls and says “I need an appointment,” \[pause\] your job is to determine the right \[emphasis\] appointment type \[pause\] for that clinic encounter. \[pause\] New or established? \[pause\] Routine or urgent? \[pause\] Wellness visit or physical? \[pause\] Referral needed? \[pause\]

\[matter-of-fact\] The right appointment type \[pause\] sets up the provider for the right time, \[pause\] the billing team for the right code, \[pause\] and the patient for the right experience. \[pause\]

\[encouraging\] You’re not just booking a slot. \[pause\] You’re making a clinical and financial decision \[pause\] that drives the entire downstream workflow. \[pause\]

\[pause\]

\[warm\] Quick recap. \[pause\] An encounter is the system’s record of a patient interaction. \[pause\] MRN identifies the patient — never changes. \[pause\] FIN identifies the visit — new one every time. \[pause\] Scheduling creates a clinic encounter. \[pause\] The appointment type you apply to it drives the time, documentation, and billing. \[pause\] And other encounter types — phone, eRx, lab-only — exist outside of scheduled visits. \[pause\]

\[encouraging\] In the next lesson, we’ll follow an encounter from start to finish — \[pause\] the full encounter lifecycle. \[pause\]

\[warm\] You’re building real knowledge. Keep going. \[pause\]

**Delivery:** *Empowering close. Tight recap reinforces the encounter vs. appointment type distinction one final time.*

**Tone:** *Earned encouragement. Forward momentum into the lifecycle lesson.*
