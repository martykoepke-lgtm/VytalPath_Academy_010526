## **SCENE 2: What the Practice Management System Does**

\[instructional\] The PM system is the \[emphasis\] business engine of the practice. \[pause\] It handles everything related to running the practice as a business. \[pause\]

## **SCENE 3: What the EHR Does**

### **AVATAR SCRIPT — Copy directly into HeyGen**

\[instructional\] Now, the EHR. \[pause\] The EHR is the \[emphasis\] clinical engine. \[pause\] It handles everything related to the patient's medical care. \[pause\]

\[matter-of-fact\] Clinical documentation — \[pause\] when a provider examines a patient, they document their findings, assessment, and plan in the EHR. \[pause\] These are the clinical notes. \[pause\]

\[warm\] Bottom line: \[pause\] if it involves what happened medically — \[pause\] diagnoses, treatments, test results, prescriptions — \[pause\] that's the EHR. \[pause\]

**SCENE 1: What Is an Encounter?**

Do not speak \[pause\] or other words in brackets. Have a nice relaxed speaking cadence. Before Avatar is shown create an intro image/slide that has the name of the section. A title intro or relevant B-roll that is ambulatory healthcare related. Then transition to Avatar. Do not add any brand logos. Maintain existing style preferences.

**AVATAR SCRIPT — Copy directly into HeyGen**

\[warm\] Every time a patient interacts with a healthcare organization — \[pause\] whether they walk into the clinic, call on the phone, or have lab work ordered — \[pause\] the system creates something called an \[emphasis\] encounter. \[pause\]

\[instructional\] An encounter is the system’s record of that specific interaction. \[pause\] It captures what happened, \[pause\] who was involved, \[pause\] what was billed, \[pause\] and what needs to happen next. \[pause\]

\[matter-of-fact\] A patient can have dozens — even hundreds — of encounters over their lifetime. \[pause\] Each one is a separate record. \[pause\] They’re all tied together by the patient’s \[emphasis\] Medical Record Number — their MRN. \[pause\] One patient, one MRN, forever. \[pause\]

\[instructional\] Each encounter gets its own \[emphasis\] Financial Identification Number — the FIN. \[pause\] One visit, one FIN. \[pause\] The MRN stays the same. \[pause\] The FIN changes with every encounter. \[pause\]

**Delivery:** *Foundational. Take time with MRN vs FIN — first time many learners will hear this.*

**Tone:** *Precise but not clinical. Make it stick.*

**SCENE 2: Clinic Encounters and Appointment Types**

**AVATAR SCRIPT — Copy directly into HeyGen**

\[instructional\] When you schedule an appointment, \[pause\] the system creates a \[emphasis\] clinic encounter. \[pause\] That’s the container — the record in the EHR for that visit. \[pause\]

\[matter-of-fact\] But you don’t just create the encounter and move on. \[pause\] You apply an \[emphasis\] appointment type \[pause\] to that clinic encounter. \[pause\] New Patient. \[pause\] Established Patient. \[pause\] Medicare Annual Wellness Visit. \[pause\] Each of those is an appointment type — \[pause\] and it gets attached to the clinic encounter you just created. \[pause\]

\[instructional\] That appointment type determines everything downstream. \[pause\] How much time the provider gets. \[pause\] What documentation they complete. \[pause\] Which billing code gets used — \[pause\] and how much the practice gets paid. \[pause\]

\[emphasis\] Wrong appointment type \[pause\] means wrong time, wrong documentation, wrong billing code. \[pause\] That’s a denied claim. \[pause\] The practice doesn’t get paid. \[pause\]

\[warm\] So when you select “New Patient” versus “Established Patient” — \[pause\] that’s a billing decision. \[pause\] A compliance decision. \[pause\] And it starts with you. \[pause\]

**Delivery:** *Build the stakes gradually. Don’t scare — inform.*

**Tone:** *"This is important and you can handle it."*

**SCENE 3: Common Appointment Types**

**AVATAR SCRIPT — Copy directly into HeyGen**

\[instructional\] Let’s walk through the appointment types you’ll use most. \[pause\]

\[matter-of-fact\] \[emphasis\] New Patient — NP. \[pause\] Someone who hasn’t been seen by this provider or specialty group in three years. \[pause\] Longer slots — 30 to 60 minutes. \[pause\] Full demographic entry, insurance setup, comprehensive intake. \[pause\]

\[matter-of-fact\] \[emphasis\] Established Patient — EST. \[pause\] Seen within the past three years. \[pause\] Shorter slots — 15 to 20 minutes. \[pause\] You verify and update rather than build from scratch. \[pause\]

\[matter-of-fact\] \[emphasis\] Medicare Annual Wellness Visit — AWV. \[pause\] This trips people up. \[pause\] It is \[emphasis\] not a physical exam. \[pause\] It’s a health risk assessment and prevention plan. \[pause\] Medicare covers it once per year. \[pause\] Schedule it as a regular physical and the claim gets denied. \[pause\] Make sure the appointment type and the patient’s expectations match. \[pause\]

\[matter-of-fact\] \[emphasis\] Transitional Care Management — TCM. \[pause\] Follow-up after a hospital or facility discharge. \[pause\] Seven days for complex, fourteen for moderate. \[pause\] Your job: schedule within that window and confirm the discharge date. \[pause\]

\[matter-of-fact\] Others you’ll see: \[pause\] \[emphasis\] Preventive visits — the annual physical. \[pause\] \[emphasis\] Procedure visits — injections, biopsies, minor surgeries. \[pause\] \[emphasis\] Consults — specialist referral visits. \[pause\] \[emphasis\] Pre-ops — surgical clearance. \[pause\] And \[emphasis\] urgent or same-day visits \[pause\] squeezed into held-open slots. \[pause\]
