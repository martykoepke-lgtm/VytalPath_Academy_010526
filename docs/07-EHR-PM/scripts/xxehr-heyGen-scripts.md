## **SCENE 2: Name the Systems**

# **VIDEO 2: PRACTICE MANAGEMENT vs EHR — UNDERSTANDING YOUR SYSTEMS**

**Video Type:** Core Lesson (Instructional)
**Duration:** 5–6 minutes
**Tone:** Clear, practical, conversational
**Placement:** Module 1 lesson — could replace or supplement the reading lesson `pm-vs-ehr`

---

## **SCENE 1: The Two Systems in Every Clinic**

### **AVATAR SCRIPT — Copy directly into HeyGen**

\[warm\] Walk into almost any medical office in the country, \[pause\] and there are two software systems running behind the scenes. \[pause\] Every schedule, every patient record, every insurance claim, every message — \[pause\] it all flows through these two systems. \[pause\]

\[instructional\] The first is the \[emphasis\] Practice Management system, \[pause\] or PM. \[pause\] The second is the \[emphasis\] Electronic Health Record, \[pause\] or EHR. \[pause\]

\[matter-of-fact\] Some clinics have them as one integrated platform. \[pause\] Some have them as separate systems that talk to each other. \[pause\] But conceptually, they handle two different sides of the business. \[pause\]

\[warm\] And as front office staff, \[pause\] you need to understand both. \[pause\]

### **PRODUCTION NOTES**

* Delivery: Set the scene. Every learner should feel like this is the missing context they've needed.
* Tone: Grounding, practical. "This is how every clinic works."

---

## **SCENE 2: What the Practice Management System Does**

### **AVATAR SCRIPT — Copy directly into HeyGen**

\[instructional\] The PM system is the \[emphasis\] business engine of the practice. \[pause\] It handles everything related to running the practice as a business. \[pause\]

\[matter-of-fact\] Scheduling — \[pause\] this is where you build the day. \[pause\] You see open slots, book appointments, manage cancellations and reschedules. \[pause\]

Patient demographics — \[pause\] name, address, phone number, emergency contact, employer. \[pause\] This is where that information lives and gets updated. \[pause\]

Insurance information — \[pause\] the subscriber, the plan, the group number, the authorization. \[pause\] When you verify eligibility, you're working in the PM. \[pause\]

Billing and claims — \[pause\] once the provider finishes with a patient, charges flow into the PM, \[pause\] claims get built and submitted, payments get posted. \[pause\]

And reporting — \[pause\] daily reconciliation, accounts receivable, no-show tracking. \[pause\] The PM generates the business intelligence the practice needs to operate. \[pause\]

\[warm\] Bottom line: \[pause\] if it involves scheduling, money, or patient contact information — \[pause\] you're in the PM. \[pause\]

### **PRODUCTION NOTES**

* Delivery: Methodical walkthrough. Each function gets its own beat.
* Tone: Like giving a tour of a building — "This is the scheduling wing, this is the billing department."

---

## **SCENE 3: What the EHR Does**

### **AVATAR SCRIPT — Copy directly into HeyGen**

\[instructional\] Now, the EHR. \[pause\] The EHR is the \[emphasis\] clinical engine. \[pause\] It handles everything related to the patient's medical care. \[pause\]

\[matter-of-fact\] Clinical documentation — \[pause\] when a provider examines a patient, they document their findings, assessment, and plan in the EHR. \[pause\] These are the clinical notes. \[pause\]

Orders — \[pause\] lab orders, imaging orders, referral orders. \[pause\] When a provider says "I need a CBC" or "Let's get an MRI" — \[pause\] that order is created and tracked in the EHR. \[pause\]

Results — \[pause\] when lab results come back, when imaging reports are finalized — \[pause\] they flow into the EHR and appear in the patient's record. \[pause\]

Medications — \[pause\] the medication list, allergies, and electronic prescriptions. \[pause\] When a provider sends a prescription to the pharmacy, that's the EHR. \[pause\]

And clinical messaging — \[pause\] message pools, task lists, nurse triage queues. \[pause\] When you route a patient's phone message to the nurse — \[pause\] you're putting that message into the EHR. \[pause\]

\[warm\] Bottom line: \[pause\] if it involves what happened medically — \[pause\] diagnoses, treatments, test results, prescriptions — \[pause\] that's the EHR. \[pause\]

### **PRODUCTION NOTES**

* Delivery: Mirror the PM walkthrough structure. Same pace, same rhythm.
* Tone: Parallel construction reinforces the distinction between the two systems.

---

## **SCENE 4: How They Connect**

### **AVATAR SCRIPT — Copy directly into HeyGen**

\[instructional\] Now here's where it gets interesting. \[pause\] These two systems don't work in isolation. \[pause\] They pass information back and forth all day long. \[pause\]

\[matter-of-fact\] The bridge between them \[pause\] is the \[emphasis\] encounter. \[pause\]

When you schedule an appointment in the PM, \[pause\] the PM creates a scheduling container — \[pause\] a time slot, a patient, a provider, an encounter type. \[pause\]

When that patient arrives and is checked in, \[pause\] the encounter \[emphasis\] opens in the EHR. \[pause\] Now the clinical side can document. \[pause\]

When the provider finishes and selects their billing codes, \[pause\] those charges \[emphasis\] flow back to the PM \[pause\] where the claim is built and submitted. \[pause\]

\[instructional\] So the encounter starts in the PM, \[pause\] passes through the EHR, \[pause\] and comes back to the PM for billing. \[pause\] That's the cycle. \[pause\]

\[warm\] And as front desk, \[pause\] you're at both ends of that cycle. \[pause\] You start the encounter with scheduling and check-in, \[pause\] and you close it with check-out, follow-up, and billing support. \[pause\]

### **PRODUCTION NOTES**

* Delivery: This is the "aha" moment. Build to the encounter as the connecting concept.
* Tone: Connecting the dots. Make the learner see the full picture.

## **SCENE 5: Your Daily Reality**

### **AVATAR SCRIPT — Copy directly into HeyGen**

Their encounter opens for the provider — \[emphasis\] EHR. \[pause\] You don't do the clinical work, but you can see the status change. \[pause\]

A patient calls with a medication question. \[pause\] You take the message and route it to the nurse pool — \[emphasis\] EHR. \[pause\]

Another patient needs to reschedule. \[pause\] You pull up the schedule — \[emphasis\] PM. \[pause\]

End of day, you run your reports — \[emphasis\] PM. \[pause\] Reconcile copays, track no-shows, review tomorrow's schedule. \[pause\]

\[warm\] You're bouncing between these two systems all day. \[pause\] But once you understand what each one does, \[pause\] it feels natural. \[pause\] Like knowing which tool to reach for. \[pause\]

# **VIDEO 3: ENCOUNTERS & ENCOUNTER SELECTION**

**Video Type:** Core Lesson (Instructional)
**Duration:** 6–7 minutes
**Tone:** Practical, precise, stakes-aware
**Placement:** Module 2 opener — could supplement or replace reading lesson `clinic-encounter-types`

---

## **SCENE 1: What Is an Encounter?**

### **AVATAR SCRIPT — Copy directly into HeyGen**

\[warm\] Every time a patient interacts with a healthcare organization — \[pause\] whether they walk into the clinic, call on the phone, or have lab work ordered — \[pause\] the system creates something called an \[emphasis\] encounter. \[pause\]

\[instructional\] An encounter is the system's record of that specific interaction. \[pause\] It captures what happened, \[pause\] who was involved, \[pause\] what was billed, \[pause\] and what needs to happen next. \[pause\]

\[matter-of-fact\] Think of it this way. \[pause\] A patient can have dozens — even hundreds — of encounters over their lifetime. \[pause\] Each one is a separate record. \[pause\] A separate story. \[pause\] But they're all tied together by one thing: \[pause\] the patient's \[emphasis\] Medical Record Number — their MRN. \[pause\]

\[instructional\] The MRN identifies the patient. \[pause\] One patient, one MRN, forever. \[pause\] Each encounter gets its own \[emphasis\] Financial Identification Number — the FIN. \[pause\] One visit, one FIN. \[pause\] The MRN stays the same. \[pause\] The FIN changes with every encounter. \[pause\]

### **PRODUCTION NOTES**

* Delivery: Foundational. Take time with MRN vs FIN — this is the first time many learners will hear this.
* Tone: Precise but not clinical. Make it stick.

---

## **SCENE 2: Why Encounter Type Matters**

### **AVATAR SCRIPT — Copy directly into HeyGen**

\[instructional\] Now, not all encounters are the same. \[pause\] And this is where your job gets really important. \[pause\]

\[matter-of-fact\] When you schedule an appointment, \[pause\] you don't just pick a time. \[pause\] You select an \[emphasis\] encounter type. \[pause\] And that choice \[pause\] determines everything that happens downstream. \[pause\]

\[instructional\] The encounter type determines \[pause\] how much time the provider gets with the patient. \[pause\] It determines what documentation the provider needs to complete. \[pause\] It determines which \[emphasis\] billing code gets used — \[pause\] and billing codes determine how much the practice gets paid. \[pause\]

\[emphasis\] Wrong encounter type \[pause\] equals wrong time allocation, \[pause\] wrong documentation requirements, \[pause\] and the wrong billing code. \[pause\] That means a \[emphasis\] denied claim. \[pause\] The practice doesn't get paid. \[pause\]

\[warm\] So when you're scheduling and you select "New Patient" versus "Established Patient" — \[pause\] that's not a small decision. \[pause\] That's a billing decision. \[pause\] A compliance decision. \[pause\] And it starts with you. \[pause\]

### **PRODUCTION NOTES**

* Delivery: Build the stakes gradually. Don't scare — inform.
* Tone: "This is important and you can handle it."

---

## **SCENE 3: Clinic Encounter Types**

### **AVATAR SCRIPT — Copy directly into HeyGen**

\[instructional\] Let's walk through the most common clinic encounter types. \[pause\]

\[matter-of-fact\] \[emphasis\] New Patient — or NP. \[pause\] This is someone who hasn't been seen by this provider or specialty group in the past three years. \[pause\] New patients need longer appointment slots — \[pause\] usually 30 to 60 minutes — \[pause\] because you're building their record from scratch. \[pause\] Full demographic entry, insurance setup, comprehensive intake forms. \[pause\]

\[matter-of-fact\] \[emphasis\] Established Patient — or EST. \[pause\] This is someone who has been seen within the past three years. \[pause\] Shorter check-in. \[pause\] You verify and update their information rather than entering it all new. \[pause\] Usually 15 to 20 minute slots. \[pause\]

\[pause\]

\[matter-of-fact\] \[emphasis\] Medicare Annual Wellness Visit — the AWV or MAWV. \[pause\] This one trips people up. \[pause\] An Annual Wellness Visit is \[emphasis\] not a physical exam. \[pause\] It's a health risk assessment and a personalized prevention plan. \[pause\] Medicare covers it once per year, \[pause\] and if you schedule it as a regular physical, \[pause\] the claim will be denied. \[pause\] The patient may get an unexpected bill. \[pause\] Front desk needs to make sure both the encounter type and the patient's expectations are set correctly. \[pause\]

\[pause\]

\[matter-of-fact\] \[emphasis\] Transitional Care Management — TCM. \[pause\] This is a follow-up visit for patients who were just discharged from a hospital, skilled nursing facility, or rehab. \[pause\] It has to happen within a specific window — \[pause\] seven days for complex cases, \[pause\] fourteen days for moderate. \[pause\] Front desk role: \[pause\] schedule within the required timeframe, \[pause\] confirm the discharge date, \[pause\] and document any contact attempts. \[pause\]

### **PRODUCTION NOTES**

* Delivery: Encyclopedic but not boring. Each type gets its own micro-story.
* Tone: Teacher mode. Naming things, defining things, showing why each matters.

---

## **SCENE 4: More Encounter Types**

### **AVATAR SCRIPT — Copy directly into HeyGen**

\[instructional\] A few more you'll see regularly. \[pause\]

\[matter-of-fact\] \[emphasis\] Preventive or Well Visit. \[pause\] This is the annual physical. \[pause\] Routine health maintenance. \[pause\] Different from the Medicare AWV — \[pause\] a physical includes a head-to-toe exam. \[pause\]

\[matter-of-fact\] \[emphasis\] Procedure encounters. \[pause\] Minor surgeries, injections, biopsies. \[pause\] These require specific scheduling blocks and sometimes special prep instructions for the patient. \[pause\]

\[matter-of-fact\] \[emphasis\] Consult encounters. \[pause\] When a specialist sees a patient for the first time based on a referral from another provider. \[pause\] Front desk needs to make sure the referral is on file before the visit. \[pause\]

\[matter-of-fact\] \[emphasis\] Pre-op visits. \[pause\] Clearance visits before surgery. \[pause\] These are time-sensitive — they have to happen within a specific window before the procedure date. \[pause\]

\[matter-of-fact\] And \[emphasis\] urgent or same-day visits. \[pause\] Worked into the schedule when a patient calls with an acute issue. \[pause\] These get squeezed in — \[pause\] usually in held-open slots or through double-booking. \[pause\]

### **PRODUCTION NOTES**

* Delivery: Brisk but complete. These are supporting types — important but don't need the depth of NP/EST/AWV.
* Tone: Efficient. Building vocabulary.

---

## **SCENE 5: Beyond the Clinic — Other Encounter Types**

### **AVATAR SCRIPT — Copy directly into HeyGen**

\[instructional\] Not every encounter happens face-to-face. \[pause\]

\[matter-of-fact\] \[emphasis\] Phone encounters. \[pause\] When a clinical decision is made over the phone — \[pause\] triage advice, medication changes, test results discussed — \[pause\] a phone encounter is created in the EHR. \[pause\] Not every call needs one. \[pause\] Scheduling calls and billing questions don't. \[pause\] But anything involving clinical information does. \[pause\]

\[matter-of-fact\] \[emphasis\] eRx encounters. \[pause\] When a provider sends a prescription without seeing the patient — \[pause\] a refill authorization, a dose adjustment after reviewing labs. \[pause\]

\[matter-of-fact\] \[emphasis\] Lab-only and imaging-only encounters. \[pause\] Provider orders labs or imaging without an office visit. \[pause\] The patient goes directly to the lab or imaging center. \[pause\]

\[matter-of-fact\] And \[emphasis\] prior authorization encounters. \[pause\] Some practices create a tracking encounter for the prior auth process — \[pause\] request submitted, payer contacted, approved or denied. \[pause\]

\[warm\] You won't create all of these yourself. \[pause\] But you need to know they exist \[pause\] because patients will ask about them, \[pause\] providers will reference them, \[pause\] and you'll see them in the system. \[pause\]

### **PRODUCTION NOTES**

* Delivery: Broadening the picture. Moving beyond "appointment = encounter."
* Tone: Expanding awareness without overwhelming.

## **SCENE 6: The Front Desk Decision**

### **AVATAR SCRIPT — Copy directly into HeyGen**

\[warm\] So let's bring this back to you. \[pause\]

\[instructional\] When a patient calls and says "I need an appointment," \[pause\] your job isn't just to find an open slot. \[pause\] Your job is to determine the right \[emphasis\] encounter type. \[pause\]

\[matter-of-fact\] Is this a new patient or established? \[pause\] Is this a routine visit or something urgent? \[pause\] Does the provider need a long slot or a short one? \[pause\] Is this a Medicare wellness visit or an annual physical? \[pause\] Does the patient need a referral on file before they can be seen? \[pause\]

\[instructional\] Each of these questions leads you to the correct encounter type. \[pause\] And the correct encounter type \[pause\] sets up the provider for the right amount of time, \[pause\] the billing team for the right code, \[pause\] and the patient for the right experience. \[pause\]

\[encouraging\] You're not just booking a slot on a calendar. \[pause\] You're making a clinical and financial decision \[pause\] that affects the entire downstream workflow. \[pause\]

### **PRODUCTION NOTES**

* Delivery: Empowering. Elevate the learner's understanding of their role.
* Tone: "This is why you matter." Not generic motivation — specific, earned.
