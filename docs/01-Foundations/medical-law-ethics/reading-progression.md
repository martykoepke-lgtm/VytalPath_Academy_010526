# Medical Law & Ethics: Reading Progression Guide

## Overview

This guide provides the recommended order for reading the Medical Law & Ethics materials. Each topic builds on the previous one, creating a logical progression from basic concepts to more complex rules.

---

## Reading Order

### 1. Start Here: HIPAA Essentials (Video)
**What you learn:** What HIPAA is and why it exists.
**Why first:** Everything else builds on this foundation.

---

### 2. PHI Explained (Video)
**What you learn:** What counts as protected health information.
**Why second:** You need to know what you're protecting before you learn the rules.

---

### 3. HIPAA Violations, Fines & Penalties (Reading)
**What you learn:** What happens when HIPAA is broken. The four penalty tiers. Real cases.
**Why third:** Understanding consequences makes the rules meaningful.

*Source: 45 CFR § 160.404, 42 U.S.C. § 1320d-6*

---

### 4. The Minimum Necessary Standard (Reading)
**What you learn:** Only access and share what you need. No more.
**Why fourth:** This is the practical rule you'll use every day.

*Source: 45 CFR § 164.502(b)*

---

### 5. Patient Rights Under HIPAA (Reading)
**What you learn:** The 6 rights patients have over their information.
**Why fifth:** You'll help patients use these rights. You need to know them.

*Source: 45 CFR §§ 164.520-164.528*

---

### 6. Authorization & Consent (Reading)
**What you learn:** When you need special permission to share information.
**Why sixth:** Builds on patient rights — now you learn when you need their okay.

*Source: 45 CFR §§ 164.506, 164.508*

---

### 7. EMTALA: The Patient Anti-Dumping Law (Reading)
**What you learn:** Emergency care rules. Screen, stabilize, transfer safely.
**Why seventh:** Shifts focus from privacy to emergency care. Important if you work near an ER.

*Source: 42 U.S.C. § 1395dd, 42 CFR § 489.24*

---

### 8. Healthcare Fraud, Abuse & Stark Law (Reading)
**What you learn:** Billing fraud, kickbacks, and self-referral rules.
**Why last:** More advanced topic. Good to know the red flags and how to protect yourself.

*Source: 31 U.S.C. §§ 3729-3733, 42 U.S.C. § 1320a-7b, 42 U.S.C. § 1395nn*

---

## Tips for Learning

1. **Don't rush.** Take breaks between topics if you need to.
2. **Take notes.** Write down things that surprise you or that you want to remember.
3. **Think of examples.** For each rule, imagine a real situation where it would apply.
4. **Ask questions.** If something doesn't make sense, mark it and ask your supervisor.
5. **Come back later.** These materials are references. You can revisit them anytime.

---

## After Reading

Once you've completed all the reading materials:
1. Take the interactive experiences (Simulation, Spot the Violation, Risk Meter)
2. Review any topics where you had questions
3. Celebrate — you've built a strong foundation in healthcare compliance!
