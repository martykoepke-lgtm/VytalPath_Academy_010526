**CMS \= Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services**

It's the federal agency that runs Medicare (65+ and disabled) and oversees state Medicaid programs. For medical administrative assistants, "CMS billing and documentation requirements" means the **rules you must follow to get paid by these government programs**.

## **What CMAA Candidates Need to Know About CMS Requirements**

### **1\. Documentation Standards**

**"If it wasn't documented, it didn't happen"**

* Every service billed must have supporting documentation in the medical record  
* Provider must sign/date all notes  
* Documentation must justify the level of service billed (E/M codes)  
* Corrections must be made properly (single line through error, initialed/dated \- never white out)

**Medical Necessity:**

* Services must be "reasonable and necessary" for diagnosis/treatment  
* Diagnosis code (ICD-10) must support procedure code (CPT)  
* Example: Can't bill knee X-ray for patient with sore throat

### **2\. Timely Filing Requirements**

* **Medicare:** 1 year from date of service (12 months)  
* **Medicaid:** Varies by state (often 90 days to 1 year)  
* Miss the deadline \= claim denied, can't bill patient

### **3\. Advanced Beneficiary Notice (ABN)**

**When Medicare might NOT cover a service:**

* Must give patient written notice BEFORE service  
* Patient chooses: proceed anyway and pay out-of-pocket, or decline service  
* Without proper ABN, practice can't bill patient if Medicare denies

**Common ABN scenarios:**

* Screening tests done too frequently  
* Services deemed "not medically necessary"  
* Experimental/investigational treatments

### **4\. Coordination of Benefits (COB)**

**When patient has multiple insurance:**

* Medicare is usually **secondary** to employer group coverage (if still working and employer has 20+ employees)  
* Medicaid is almost always **last payer**  
* Must bill in correct order or claims get denied

### **5\. National Correct Coding Initiative (NCCI)**

**CMS rules on what can/cannot be billed together:**

* Certain procedure codes are "bundled" (can't bill separately)  
* Example: Can't bill separately for giving an injection AND the office visit that included it  
* Prevents "unbundling" fraud

### **6\. Modifier Usage**

**Two-digit codes that provide additional claim information:**

* **\-25**: Significant, separately identifiable E/M service on same day as procedure  
* **\-59**: Distinct procedural service  
* **\-76**: Repeat procedure by same physician  
* **\-GA**: ABN on file (waiver of liability)

Wrong modifier \= claim denial

### **7\. Place of Service (POS) Codes**

Must match where service actually occurred:

* **11** \= Office  
* **12** \= Home  
* **21** \= Inpatient hospital  
* **22** \= Outpatient hospital  
* **23** \= Emergency room

### **8\. National Provider Identifier (NPI) Requirements**

* All providers must have NPI on claims  
* Individual NPI (Type 1\) and organizational NPI (Type 2\)  
* Billing without proper NPI \= immediate rejection

* Decline service

**Critical Rule:** Without proper ABN on file, practice cannot bill patient if Medicare denies claim

### **COORDINATION OF BENEFITS (COB)**

When patient has multiple insurance coverage, must bill in correct order:

**Primary vs Secondary Payer Rules:**

* Medicare is SECONDARY to employer group coverage if patient still working and employer has 20+ employees  
* Medicare is PRIMARY for retirees, disabled under 65, ESRD patients (after 30-month coordination period)  
* Medicaid is almost always LAST payer (payer of last resort)  
* Must bill primary insurance first, then submit EOB to secondary with claim

**Birthday Rule for Dependents:**

* Parent with birthday earlier in calendar year \= primary coverage  
* If same birthday, parent who has had coverage longer \= primary

### **NATIONAL CORRECT CODING INITIATIVE (NCCI)**

CMS rules governing what procedure codes can and cannot be billed together on same date of service.

**Purpose:** Prevent "unbundling" fraud where providers bill separately for services that should be reported together

**Examples:**

* Cannot bill separately for giving an injection AND the office visit that included it  
* Certain procedures include routine pre-op and post-op care (cannot bill separately)  
* Some diagnostic tests include interpretation (cannot bill separately)

**Medical Assistant Role:** Check NCCI edits before claim submission to prevent denials

### **MODIFIER USAGE**

Two-digit codes added to CPT codes that provide additional information about service performed.

**Common Modifiers MAs Encounter:**

* Modifier 25: Significant, separately identifiable Evaluation and Management service on same day as procedure  
* Modifier 59: Distinct procedural service (performed at different session, different site, separate incision)  
* Modifier 76: Repeat procedure by same physician on same day  
* Modifier 77: Repeat procedure by different physician on same day  
* Modifier GA: Waiver of liability statement (ABN) on file  
* Modifier GZ: Item/service expected to be denied as not reasonable and necessary

**Critical Rule:** Incorrect or missing modifier \= claim denial

### **PLACE OF SERVICE (POS) CODES**

Must accurately reflect where service was performed:

* 11 \= Office  
* 12 \= Home  
* 21 \= Inpatient Hospital  
* 22 \= Outpatient Hospital  
* 23 \= Emergency Room  
* 81 \= Independent Laboratory

**Why It Matters:** Medicare pays different amounts based on POS, incorrect code \= claim rejection or audit

### **NATIONAL PROVIDER IDENTIFIER (NPI) REQUIREMENTS**

* All providers and organizations must have NPI on claims  
* Type 1 NPI \= Individual provider (physicians, NPs, PAs)  
* Type 2 NPI \= Organizational provider (hospitals, clinics, group practices)  
* Billing without proper NPI \= immediate claim rejection  
* NPI must match provider who performed service (cannot bill under wrong NPI)

### **MEDICARE VS MEDICAID BASICS**

**Medicare:**

* Federal program  
* Primarily for 65+ and disabled  
* Part A \= Hospital insurance  
* Part B \= Medical insurance (doctor visits, outpatient)  
* Part C \= Medicare Advantage (private plan alternative)  
* Part D \= Prescription drug coverage  
* Accepts assignment \= provider agrees to Medicare-approved amount

**Medicaid:**

* State and federal program  
* For low-income individuals and families  
* Eligibility varies by state  
* Covers services Medicare doesn't (long-term care, personal care)  
* Most providers must be enrolled as Medicaid providers to bill

**Medigap (Medicare Supplement):**

* Private insurance that covers Medicare deductibles and coinsurance  
* Billed after Medicare processes primary claim

**TRICARE:**

* Military health coverage  
* For active duty, retirees, and dependents  
* Has own billing rules separate from Medicare/Medicaid

### **COMMON CMS COMPLIANCE VIOLATIONS MEDICAL ASSISTANTS HELP PREVENT**

**Upcoding:**

* Billing for higher level service than documented or provided  
* Example: Billing Level 4 office visit when documentation only supports Level 3  
* MA Prevention: Verify documentation supports codes before claim submission

**Unbundling:**

* Billing separately for services that should be reported together  
* Example: Billing for office visit, blood draw, and lab processing when only comprehensive code should be used  
* MA Prevention: Check NCCI edits and use bundled codes appropriately

**Duplicate Billing:**

* Submitting same claim multiple times  
* MA Prevention: Review claim history before resubmitting denied claims

**Lack of Medical Necessity:**

* Diagnosis code doesn't support procedure billed  
* MA Prevention: Ensure appropriate diagnosis codes are present and linked to procedures

**Missing Signatures:**

* Provider didn't sign documentation  
* MA Prevention: Verify all notes are signed before billing goes out

**Incorrect Patient Information:**

* Wrong Medicare ID, misspelled name, incorrect date of birth  
* MA Prevention: Verify patient demographics match insurance card exactly

### **COMMON PITFALLS TO AVOID**

**Academic Pitfalls:**

* Curriculum not aligned with current CMAA exam blueprint  
* Assessments too easy (students pass program but fail CMAA)  
* Insufficient hands-on practice  
* Outdated content (especially technology and regulations)  
* No instructor feedback mechanism

**Business Pitfalls:**

* Unclear refund policy leading to disputes  
* Over-promising outcomes (guaranteed job placement)  
* Under-documenting student records (can't verify completion)  
* No grievance process (students have no recourse)  
* Inadequate liability insurance for externships

**Marketing Pitfalls:**

* Claiming accreditation you don't have  
* Inflating job prospects or salaries  
* Not disclosing program limitations  
* Testimonials from non-graduates  
* Bait-and-switch pricing

**Operational Pitfalls:**

* No backup plan if instructor unavailable  
* Inadequate technology support for students  
* Lost student records due to poor data management  
* Externship sites backing out with no alternatives  
* No process for handling student complaints
