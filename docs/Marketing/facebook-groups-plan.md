# Facebook Groups Marketing Plan

## VytalPath Academy — Healthcare Front Office Training

---

## Strategy Overview

Facebook Groups are the single best free marketing channel for a niche product like VytalPath. The people you want to reach — career changers, job seekers, healthcare workers, and clinic managers — are already in groups talking about these exact topics.

**Goal:** Build authority, provide genuine value, and organically drive signups. No spamming. No cold pitching. People buy from people they trust.

**Cost:** $0

**Time commitment:** 30-45 minutes/day, 5 days/week

---

## Phase 2: Establish Presence (Weeks 1-2)

**Do NOT post about VytalPath yet.** Spend 2 weeks being a helpful member.

### Daily Actions:
1. **Comment on 3-5 posts per day** in different groups. Give genuinely helpful answers to questions about healthcare careers, job searching, or front office work.
2. **React to posts** — likes and reactions increase your visibility in the group.
3. **Introduce yourself** if groups have intro threads: "I work in healthcare informatics and training. Excited to be here!"

### Example helpful comments:

**When someone asks "How do I get into healthcare with no experience?"**
> Great question! Healthcare front office roles (medical receptionist, patient access, scheduling coordinator) are one of the best entry points. Most clinics will train you on their specific EHR system, but they want to see that you understand HIPAA basics, insurance verification, and professional phone etiquette. Focus on those three areas and you'll stand out from other applicants. Happy to share more details if you're interested!

**When someone asks "What does a medical receptionist do?"**
> Day to day it's a mix of: checking patients in and out, verifying insurance eligibility, scheduling appointments, answering phones (a LOT of phones), handling referrals and prior authorizations, and entering data into the EHR system. It varies by clinic size — at a small practice you'll do everything, at a big hospital system you might specialize in one area like scheduling or registration.

**When someone asks "Is medical billing and coding worth it?"**
> Billing and coding is a solid field, but a lot of people overlook front office roles — medical receptionist, patient access rep, referral coordinator. They're in high demand, often easier to break into, and many of the skills overlap (insurance knowledge, medical terminology, EHR navigation). Worth considering if you want to get your foot in the door faster.

## Phase 3: Value Posts (Weeks 3+)

Now that people recognize your name, start posting original content. The ratio should be **80% value / 20% promotion**. Most of your posts should NOT mention VytalPath at all.

### Value Post Templates (No Promotion)

**Post Type: Quick Tip**
> 💡 Quick tip for anyone interviewing for a medical receptionist position:
>
> When they ask "Do you have EHR experience?" — don't just say no. Say something like:
>
> "I haven't worked in [Epic/Athena/specific system] yet, but I understand the core workflows — patient registration, appointment scheduling, checking insurance eligibility, and documenting encounters. I'm a fast learner with new software."
>
> Hiring managers care more about whether you understand the PROCESS than whether you've clicked the exact same buttons before. Every EHR is different, but the workflows are universal.

**Post Type: Industry Insight**
> Something I see a lot in healthcare hiring: clinics struggle to find front office staff who understand insurance verification.
>
> It's not glamorous, but if you can confidently explain the difference between a copay, deductible, and coinsurance — and you know how to check eligibility — you're already ahead of 80% of applicants.
>
> If you're looking to break into healthcare, insurance basics is the highest-ROI skill you can learn.

**Post Type: Day in the Life**
> Ever wonder what a day at a medical front desk actually looks like? Here's a typical morning:
>
> 7:45 — Arrive, boot up systems, check the day's schedule for any cancellations or double-books
> 8:00 — First patients arrive. Check them in: verify demographics, scan insurance card, collect copay
> 8:30 — Phone starts ringing. Mix of appointment requests, prescription refill calls, and "can I speak to my doctor?"
> 9:00 — New patient arrives without completing paperwork online. Walk them through registration forms
> 9:30 — Referral comes in from another clinic. Enter it, verify insurance authorization
> 10:00 — Patient checkout. Schedule follow-up, print after-visit summary
>
> And that's just the first two hours. It's fast-paced, but once you know the workflows, it becomes second nature.

**Post Type: Question/Engagement**
> For anyone working (or wanting to work) in a medical office — what's the one thing you wish you'd known before your first day?
>
> I'll go first: I wish I'd known how much of the job is insurance-related. Nobody tells you that 40% of your day is verifying coverage, calling payers, and dealing with prior authorizations.

## Phase 4: Relationship Building (Ongoing)

### DM Strategy
When someone engages with your posts (likes, comments, asks a question), send a low-pressure DM:

> Hey [Name]! Saw your comment on the [healthcare career / front office] post. Are you looking to get into healthcare, or are you already working in the field?

Then have a real conversation. Don't pitch unless they express interest or ask.

### Group Admin Relationships
- Identify the 5 most active and relevant groups
- Build a relationship with the admins by being a consistent, helpful member
- After a month, DM the admin: "Hey, I run a healthcare training platform. Would you be open to me doing a free Q&A post or live session for the group about breaking into healthcare front office work?"
- If they say yes, do a genuinely helpful session and mention VytalPath naturally

## Rules to Follow

1. **Read group rules before posting.** Some groups ban all promotion. Respect that — your comments still build your profile visibility.
2. **Never copy-paste the same post to multiple groups.** Facebook detects this and suppresses reach. Rewrite each post slightly.
3. **Don't post and ghost.** Respond to every comment on your posts. Engagement signals tell Facebook to show your post to more people.
4. **Don't argue.** If someone challenges you or is negative, be gracious. "That's a fair point — thanks for sharing your perspective."
5. **Your personal profile matters.** Make sure your Facebook profile looks professional. People will click through. Have a clear bio that mentions healthcare training.
6. **Never hard-sell in comments.** If someone asks about training, say "I actually built a platform for this — happy to share the link if you're interested." Wait for them to say yes.

## Measuring Success

Track weekly:
- Number of groups you're active in
- Number of comments/posts per week
- Number of DMs received
- Number of link clicks (use UTM: `?utm_source=fbgroups&utm_campaign=value_post`)
- Number of signups attributed to Facebook

**Realistic expectations:**
- Weeks 1-2: Zero signups. You're building trust.
- Weeks 3-4: First DMs and link clicks.
- Month 2: First 2-5 signups from organic group activity.
- Month 3+: Compounding. Your posts get more reach as your engagement history grows.
