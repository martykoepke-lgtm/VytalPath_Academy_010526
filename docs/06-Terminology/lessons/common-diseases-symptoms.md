# What You'll Hear at the Front Desk

## A Practical Guide to Common Diseases & Symptoms

**Concept:** Every phone call is a puzzle. Patients describe what they're feeling—your job is to recognize patterns, assess urgency, and route appropriately. This lesson teaches you the language of symptoms so you can be the communication gateway your clinic needs.

---

## How This Lesson Works

You're going to learn symptoms the way you'll actually encounter them: **through patient calls.**

Each body system includes:
- 🎧 **What patients say** — Real phrases you'll hear
- 🔍 **What it might mean** — The medical translation
- 🚨 **Red flags** — When to escalate immediately
- ✅ **Routine signs** — What can wait for a callback

**Your goal isn't diagnosis.** That's the provider's job. Your goal is **smart routing**—getting the right information to the right people at the right speed.

## ❤️ Cardiovascular System
*The heart and blood vessels*

<!-- IMAGE SUGGESTION: Simple heart icon, minimalist red/pink -->

### What Patients Say → What It Might Mean

| They Say... | Medical Term | What's Happening |
|-------------|--------------|------------------|
| "My heart is racing" | Tachycardia | Fast heart rate (>100 bpm) |
| "My heart feels like it's skipping" | Palpitations / Arrhythmia | Irregular heartbeat |
| "I have chest pressure" | Angina | Reduced blood flow to heart |
| "My ankles are swollen" | Edema | Fluid retention (possible heart issue) |
| "I get dizzy when I stand up" | Orthostatic hypotension | Blood pressure drop |
| "I'm always tired and out of breath" | Possible CHF symptoms | Heart not pumping efficiently |

### 🚨 RED FLAGS — Escalate Immediately

> **"I'm having chest pain with pain going down my arm"**
> **"I can't catch my breath and my chest feels tight"**
> **"I feel like I'm going to pass out"**

These could indicate a **heart attack (MI)** or other cardiac emergency. Don't put them on hold. Get clinical staff NOW or direct to 911.

### ✅ Routine — Can Wait for Callback

- "I'd like my blood pressure medication refilled"
- "My ankles have been a little puffy for a few days"
- "I want to discuss my cholesterol results"

### Common Conditions You'll Schedule

| Abbreviation | Condition | What It Is |
|--------------|-----------|------------|
| HTN | Hypertension | High blood pressure |
| CHF | Congestive Heart Failure | Heart doesn't pump effectively |
| CAD | Coronary Artery Disease | Blocked heart arteries |
| AFib | Atrial Fibrillation | Irregular heart rhythm |

## 🧠 Neurological System
*The brain, spinal cord, and nerves*

<!-- IMAGE SUGGESTION: Simple brain icon, minimalist purple -->

### What Patients Say → What It Might Mean

| They Say... | Medical Term | What's Happening |
|-------------|--------------|------------------|
| "I have the worst headache of my life" | Thunderclap headache | Sudden severe headache |
| "One side of my face is drooping" | Facial paralysis | Possible stroke sign |
| "My arm went numb and tingly" | Paresthesia | Nerve issue |
| "I'm having trouble finding words" | Aphasia | Language processing issue |
| "I've been feeling confused" | Altered mental status | Brain function change |
| "I keep getting dizzy" | Vertigo / Dizziness | Balance or inner ear issue |

### 🚨 RED FLAGS — Escalate Immediately

**Remember F.A.S.T. for stroke:**
> **F** — Face drooping
> **A** — Arm weakness
> **S** — Speech difficulty
> **T** — Time to call 911

Also escalate:
> **"Worst headache of my life"** (could be brain bleed)
> **"I'm suddenly confused and don't know where I am"**
> **"I had a seizure"** (if new or unusual)

### ✅ Routine — Can Wait for Callback

- "I get migraines and need a refill"
- "I've had a mild headache for a couple days"
- "I'd like to discuss my memory concerns"

### Common Conditions You'll Schedule

| Abbreviation | Condition | What It Is |
|--------------|-----------|------------|
| CVA | Cerebrovascular Accident | Stroke |
| TIA | Transient Ischemic Attack | "Mini-stroke" |
| HA | Headache | Head pain |
| Migraine | Migraine | Severe recurring headache |

| They Say... | Medical Term | What's Happening |
|-------------|--------------|------------------|
| "I have terrible heartburn" | GERD / Acid reflux | Stomach acid backing up |
| "I've been throwing up all day" | Vomiting / Emesis | Stomach expelling contents |
| "I haven't had a bowel movement in days" | Constipation | Slow digestive movement |
| "I have diarrhea and stomach cramps" | Gastroenteritis | Stomach/intestinal irritation |
| "My stomach is bloated and painful" | Abdominal distension | Swelling in abdomen |
| "I noticed blood in my stool" | Hematochezia / Melena | GI bleeding |

### 🚨 RED FLAGS — Escalate Immediately

> **"I haven't been able to keep anything down for 24+ hours"**

Severe abdominal pain can indicate appendicitis, bowel obstruction, or other surgical emergencies.

### ✅ Routine — Can Wait for Callback

- "I've had some indigestion after meals"
- "I'd like to discuss my acid reflux medication"
- "I need to schedule a colonoscopy"

|-------------|--------------|------------------|
| "My knee is swollen and stiff" | Joint effusion / Arthritis | Fluid or inflammation in joint |
| "I threw out my back" | Acute back strain | Muscle or ligament injury |
| "My joints ache, especially in the morning" | Arthralgia | Joint pain (possible RA) |
| "I heard a pop and now I can't move it" | Possible ligament tear | Soft tissue injury |
| "My shoulder has been hurting for weeks" | Chronic pain / Tendinitis | Ongoing inflammation |

### 🚨 RED FLAGS — Escalate Immediately

> **"I have severe back pain with numbness in my legs"** (possible spinal issue)
> **"I can't move my arm after an injury"**

Obvious deformity, inability to bear weight, or neurological symptoms with injury need urgent evaluation.

### ✅ Routine — Can Wait for Callback

- "My arthritis has been acting up"
- "I'd like a referral to physical therapy"
- "I need to refill my anti-inflammatory"

## The Front Desk Triage Mindset

### Your Mental Checklist

When a patient describes symptoms, quickly assess:

1. **Is this life-threatening?** → 911 or immediate clinical handoff
2. **Is this urgent?** → Same-day callback or appointment
3. **Is this routine?** → Standard scheduling or callback queue

### Words That Should Raise Your Antenna

| Hear This... | Think This... |
|--------------|---------------|
| "Worst ever" | Could be serious |
| "Sudden" | Acute onset = higher concern |
| "Can't breathe" | Airway/breathing emergency |
| "Chest pain" | Cardiac until proven otherwise |
| "Numb" or "Weak" on one side | Stroke signs |
| "Blood" (vomit, stool, cough) | Internal bleeding |
| "Confused" or "Not acting right" | Neurological emergency |
| "High fever + something else" | Possible serious infection |

### What You Document

When taking a message about symptoms:
- **What** exactly are they feeling? (Use their words)
- **When** did it start?
- **How bad** is it? (Scale of 1-10, or "worst ever")
- **What makes it better or worse?**
- **Any other symptoms** with it?

Good documentation helps clinical staff triage effectively.

## Quick Reference: Red Flags by System

| System | 🚨 Call 911 / Escalate Immediately |
|--------|-----------------------------------|
| **Heart** | Chest pain + arm/jaw pain, crushing pressure, can't breathe |
| **Lungs** | Can't breathe, lips turning blue, coughing blood |
| **Brain** | Stroke signs (FAST), worst headache ever, sudden confusion |
| **GI** | Severe abdominal pain, vomiting blood, black stools |
| **Bones** | Obvious deformity, can't bear weight, numbness with back pain |
| **Diabetes** | Blood sugar <50 or >400, shaky/sweaty/confused |
| **Infection** | High fever + stiff neck, fever + confusion, spreading rash |

## Key Takeaways

1. **You're not diagnosing** — you're routing. Know enough to get the right info to the right people.

2. **Red flags = immediate action.** When in doubt, escalate. It's better to interrupt a provider unnecessarily than to miss an emergency.

3. **Document what they say.** Their exact words help clinical staff assess urgency.

4. **Trust your instincts.** If something sounds serious, it probably deserves attention.

5. **Learn the patterns.** Over time, you'll recognize common presentations and route more confidently.

*The front desk is where emergencies are caught or missed. Your awareness saves lives.*
