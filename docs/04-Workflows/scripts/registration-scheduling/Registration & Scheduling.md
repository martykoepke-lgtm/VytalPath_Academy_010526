# **LESSON 1: New Patient Registration & Scheduling**

**Duration:** 5 minutes **Learning Objective:** Register a new patient over the phone, collect required information, run eligibility, and schedule their first appointment—while preventing duplicate records. **Workflow Reference:** WF \#4

---

## **SCRIPT**

### **\[0:00-0:30\] OPENING \- The New Patient Call**

**VISUAL:** Split screen \- left side shows front desk staff with headset, right side shows phone icon with "INCOMING CALL" indicator. Professional clinic environment.

**DISPLAY TEXT:**

New Patient Registration & Scheduling

The Call That Starts the Relationship

**NARRATOR:** "When someone calls your clinic for the first time, you're not just scheduling an appointment. You're creating a record that will follow this patient through every visit, every test, every interaction with your healthcare system.

Getting it right matters. Getting it wrong creates problems that ripple through billing, clinical care, and patient trust.

Let's walk through the complete new patient registration workflow."

---

### **\[0:30-1:05\] STEP 1 \- Determine New vs. Existing**

**VISUAL:** Phone conversation illustration with speech bubble showing the key question.

**DISPLAY TEXT:**

STEP 1: Confirm They're New

"Have you been seen at our clinic before?"

• If NO or UNSURE → Search system first  
• If YES → Transfer to existing patient workflow

**NARRATOR:** "Start every call by asking: 'Have you been seen at our clinic before?'

If they say no, or they're not sure, your next step is critical. Do NOT immediately start creating a new record. First, you search."

---

### **\[1:05-1:50\] STEP 2 \- Search to Prevent Duplicates (CRITICAL)**

**VISUAL:** Red warning banner at top. Computer screen showing patient search interface with search fields highlighted.

**DISPLAY TEXT:**

⚠️ CRITICAL: ALWAYS Search Before Creating

Search by:  
• Last Name \+ First Name \+ DOB  
• Check spelling variations (Smith/Smyth, Jon/John)  
• Try maiden names or previous married names  
• Search by phone number if unclear

If FOUND → Use existing record  
If NOT FOUND → Proceed with registration

**NARRATOR:** "This is the most important step in the entire process. Always search before creating a new record.

Duplicate records cause real harm. Medical history gets split between two charts. Insurance claims get denied. Test results go to the wrong record. Providers make decisions with incomplete information.

Search by last name, first name, and date of birth. Check spelling variations—Smith versus S-M-Y-T-H, Jon without an H versus John with an H. Try maiden names. Try the phone number.

Only when you're confident no record exists do you proceed with registration."

---

### **\[1:50-2:30\] STEP 3 \- Collect Demographics**

**VISUAL:** Form layout showing demographic fields organized in logical groups.

**DISPLAY TEXT:**

STEP 3: Collect Patient Demographics

Identity:  
• Legal full name (ask them to spell it)  
• Date of birth

Contact:  
• Address (street, city, state, ZIP)  
• Primary phone / Secondary phone  
• Email address

Emergency:  
• Emergency contact name  
• Relationship  
• Phone number

Preferences:  
• Preferred language  
• Communication preferences

**NARRATOR:** "Now collect their demographics. Ask them to spell their name—don't assume. Get their date of birth, complete address, and phone numbers.

Don't skip the emergency contact. And note their preferred language and how they'd like to receive communications.

Enter this information accurately. Typos in addresses mean missed appointment letters. Wrong phone numbers mean you can't reach them."

---

### **\[2:30-3:10\] STEP 4 \- Collect Insurance & Run Eligibility**

**VISUAL:** Insurance card graphic with fields labeled, plus eligibility check indicator.

**DISPLAY TEXT:**

STEP 4: Insurance Information

Ask: "Do you have health insurance?"

If YES, collect:  
• Insurance company name  
• Member ID / Subscriber ID  
• Group number  
• Subscriber name & DOB (if different)  
• Relationship to subscriber

Then: Run quick eligibility check  
• Is this insurance accepted?  
• Is the plan active?  
• Is the patient covered?

Remind: "Please bring your insurance card   
and photo ID to your appointment"

**NARRATOR:** "Ask if they have health insurance. If yes, collect the insurance company name, member ID, group number, and subscriber information.

Then run a quick eligibility check. Verify the insurance is accepted at your clinic, the plan is active, and this patient is covered.

If there's an issue—the plan isn't accepted or coverage isn't active—let the patient know now. It's better to have that conversation before they arrive than at check-in.

Always remind them to bring their insurance card and photo ID to the appointment."

---

### **\[3:10-3:50\] STEP 5 \- Schedule & Confirm**

**VISUAL:** Calendar/scheduling interface with appointment slot being selected. Confirmation checklist.

**DISPLAY TEXT:**

STEP 5: Schedule & Confirm

Schedule:  
• Ask: "What is the reason for your visit?"  
• Determine appointment type and length  
• New patient visits: typically 30-60 minutes  
• Offer 2-3 available options

Confirm clearly:  
"Your appointment is \[Day\], \[Date\] at \[Time\]  
with \[Provider Name\]"

Instructions:  
• Clinic address and parking  
• "Please arrive 10-15 minutes early"  
• What to bring: ID, insurance card,   
  medication list, copay amount

**NARRATOR:** "Ask the reason for their visit to determine the right appointment type. New patient visits typically need 30 to 60 minutes. Offer two or three available options and book when they confirm.

Read back the appointment clearly: 'Your appointment is Tuesday, March 15th at 10 AM with Dr. Johnson.'

Give them the clinic address, parking instructions, and tell them to arrive 10 to 15 minutes early. Let them know what to bring and the copay amount if you have it."

---

### **\[3:50-4:25\] STEP 6 \- Document & Set Reminders**

**VISUAL:** Checklist showing documentation steps with checkmarks being added.

**DISPLAY TEXT:**

STEP 6: Complete the Registration

Document immediately:  
☑ Create new patient record  
☑ Enter all demographics  
☑ Enter insurance information  
☑ Flag as "New Patient"  
☑ Add special notes (interpreter, accessibility)

Set up for success:  
☑ Set appointment reminders  
☑ Send portal invitation if applicable  
☑ Set reminder for full eligibility verification  
  (48-72 hours before visit)  
☑ Mention cancellation policy (24-hour notice)

**NARRATOR:** "Don't wait—document everything immediately while the call is fresh. Create the record, enter all demographics and insurance, flag them as a new patient.

Add any special notes—if they need an interpreter, have accessibility needs, or mentioned anything the clinical team should know.

Set up appointment reminders and send a portal invitation if your clinic uses one. Set yourself a reminder to run full eligibility verification 48 to 72 hours before their visit.

And mention the cancellation policy—24-hour notice."

---

### **\[4:25-5:00\] CLOSING \- Why Accuracy Matters**

**VISUAL:** Patient journey timeline showing how registration data flows through the system: Registration → Check-in → Visit → Billing → Follow-up.

**DISPLAY TEXT:**

Why Accuracy Matters

The record you create today follows this patient through:  
→ Every check-in  
→ Every clinical visit  
→ Every insurance claim  
→ Every follow-up

Get it right the first time.

Key Reminders:  
• ALWAYS search before creating  
• Spell names correctly  
• Verify insurance is active and accepted  
• Document everything immediately

**NARRATOR:** "The record you create in this five-minute phone call will follow this patient through every interaction with your healthcare system. Every check-in, every visit, every claim, every follow-up.

Always search before creating. Spell names correctly. Verify insurance is active and accepted. Document everything immediately.

That's new patient registration done right."

# **LESSON 2: Existing Patient Scheduling**

**Duration:** 4 minutes **Learning Objective:** Efficiently schedule appointments for established patients while verifying current information and checking for insurance changes. **Workflow Reference:** WF \#5

---

## **SCRIPT**

### **\[0:00-0:25\] OPENING \- Streamlined But Not Skipped**

**VISUAL:** Front desk staff on phone, computer screen showing patient record already pulled up.

**DISPLAY TEXT:**

Existing Patient Scheduling

Streamlined—But Don't Skip the Essentials

**NARRATOR:** "Scheduling an existing patient is faster than a new registration. Their record already exists. Their history is in the system.

But faster doesn't mean you skip steps. Every existing patient call is an opportunity to catch outdated information before it causes problems.

Here's the streamlined workflow that keeps things moving while keeping data accurate."

---

### **\[0:25-0:55\] STEP 1 \- Verify Identity**

**VISUAL:** Patient search screen with name and DOB fields highlighted.

**DISPLAY TEXT:**

STEP 1: Verify Identity

Ask: "Can I have your full name   
and date of birth?"

• Search for patient in system  
• Confirm you have the correct record  
• Check for any alerts or flags

**NARRATOR:** "Start by verifying who you're talking to. Ask for their full name and date of birth. Search the system and confirm you have the correct record open.

Check for any alerts or flags on their account—outstanding balances, special instructions, or notes from previous visits."

---

### **\[0:55-1:35\] STEP 2 \- Confirm Current Information**

**VISUAL:** Patient demographics screen with phone, address, and email fields highlighted.

**DISPLAY TEXT:**

STEP 2: Confirm Current Information

Quick verification:  
"Is your phone number still \[number\]?"  
"Is your address still \[address\]?"

• Update any changes IMMEDIATELY  
• Verify email if used for communications  
• Don't assume—ask

**NARRATOR:** "Take 15 seconds to verify their contact information. 'Is your phone number still 555-1234?' 'Is your address still 123 Main Street?'

If anything has changed, update it immediately—don't make a note to do it later. Outdated contact information means missed reminders, returned mail, and patients who don't show up because they never got the message."

---

### **\[1:35-2:20\] STEP 3 \- Ask About Insurance Changes (Critical)**

**VISUAL:** Insurance verification screen with highlighted "Last Verified" date and current insurance info.

**DISPLAY TEXT:**

STEP 3: Ask About Insurance Changes

ALWAYS ask: "Is your insurance still \[Name\]?"

If CHANGED:  
• Collect new insurance information  
• Request they bring new card to appointment  
• Run eligibility check  
• Update system immediately

If SAME but \>30 days since last visit:  
• Flag for re-verification before appointment

Insurance changes \= eligibility changes

**NARRATOR:** "This step is critical and often skipped. Always ask: 'Is your insurance still Blue Cross?'

People change jobs. Coverage lapses. Plans change at the start of the year. If you don't ask, you won't know until check-in—or worse, until a claim is denied weeks later.

If their insurance has changed, collect the new information now. Ask them to bring the new card. Run an eligibility check before you finish the call.

Even if it's the same insurance, if it's been more than 30 days since their last visit, flag the account for re-verification before their appointment."

---

### **\[2:20-2:55\] STEP 4 \- Determine Need & Schedule**

**VISUAL:** Scheduling calendar with appointment types dropdown visible.

**DISPLAY TEXT:**

STEP 4: Determine Need & Schedule

Ask: "What is the reason for your visit?"

Determine:  
• Follow-up, routine visit, or new issue?  
• Specific provider requested?  
• Any urgency?

Schedule:  
• Find available slots  
• Match appointment type and length  
• Offer 2-3 options  
• Book with correct appointment type

**NARRATOR:** "Ask the reason for their visit. Is this a follow-up, a routine visit, or a new concern? Do they need a specific provider? Is there any urgency?

Search for available slots that match what they need. Offer two or three options and book once they confirm. Make sure you select the correct appointment type—it affects how much time is blocked and how the visit is billed."

---

### **\[2:55-3:35\] STEP 5 \- Confirm & Document**

**VISUAL:** Appointment confirmation screen with reminder settings visible.

**DISPLAY TEXT:**

STEP 5: Confirm & Document

Confirm clearly:  
"Your appointment is \[Day\], \[Date\] at \[Time\]  
with \[Provider\]"

• State copay amount if known  
• Verify reminder preference (call, text, email)  
• Mention cancellation policy

Document:  
• Note any information updates made  
• Flag insurance for re-verification if needed

**NARRATOR:** "Read back the appointment clearly. State the copay amount if you have it. Verify how they'd like to receive reminders. Mention the cancellation policy.

Document everything—especially any information updates you made during the call. If you flagged insurance for re-verification, make sure that note is saved."

---

### **\[3:35-4:00\] CLOSING \- The Quick Check That Prevents Problems**

**VISUAL:** Simple checklist graphic.

**DISPLAY TEXT:**

Every Existing Patient Call

✓ Verify identity  
✓ Confirm contact information  
✓ ASK about insurance changes  
✓ Schedule appropriately  
✓ Document updates

30 seconds of verification prevents  
hours of problem-solving later.

**NARRATOR:** "An existing patient call takes just a few minutes. But those quick verification questions—confirming contact info, asking about insurance changes—prevent hours of problem-solving later.

Verify identity. Confirm information. Ask about insurance. Schedule appropriately. Document your updates.

That's existing patient scheduling done right."

# **LESSON 3: Scheduling Follow-Ups & Recalls**

**Duration:** 4 minutes **Learning Objective:** Schedule follow-up appointments based on provider instructions, handle patients who decline, and manage recall lists for ongoing care. **Workflow Reference:** WF \#6

---

## **SCRIPT**

### **\[0:00-0:25\] OPENING \- Continuity of Care**

**VISUAL:** Provider note showing "Follow-up in 3 months" instruction, with arrow pointing to calendar.

**DISPLAY TEXT:**

Scheduling Follow-Ups & Recalls

Continuity of Care Starts at the Front Desk

**NARRATOR:** "When a provider writes 'follow-up in 3 months,' someone has to make that happen. When a patient is overdue for their annual wellness visit, someone has to reach out.

That someone is you. Follow-ups and recalls are how healthcare stays continuous—how patients don't fall through the cracks.

Let's cover both workflows."

---

### **\[0:25-1:05\] FOLLOW-UPS \- Reading Provider Instructions**

**VISUAL:** Example of checkout paperwork or EHR screen showing provider follow-up instructions with key elements highlighted.

**DISPLAY TEXT:**

SCHEDULING FOLLOW-UPS

Step 1: Read Provider Instructions

Look for:  
• Time frame: "2 weeks" / "3 months" / "1 year"  
• Specific provider or department  
• Special appointment type needed  
• Any prep required (fasting labs, etc.)

Provider writes it → You make it happen

**NARRATOR:** "Follow-up scheduling usually happens at checkout. Look at the provider's instructions carefully.

What's the time frame—two weeks, three months, one year? Does the patient need to see this same provider, or can they see anyone in the department? Is there a special appointment type needed? Any prep instructions?

The provider writes the order. You make it happen."

---

### **\[1:05-1:40\] FOLLOW-UPS \- Schedule & Confirm**

**VISUAL:** Calendar showing appointment being scheduled within the specified time frame.

**DISPLAY TEXT:**

Step 2: Schedule the Follow-Up

• Open scheduling calendar  
• Find available date within requested time frame  
• Select correct appointment type and duration  
• Book with patient agreement

Step 3: Confirm with Patient

"Your follow-up is scheduled for \[Day\], \[Date\]  
at \[Time\] with \[Provider\]"

• Provide appointment card or printed confirmation  
• Set reminder preferences  
• Note any prep instructions

**NARRATOR:** "Open the calendar and find an available slot within the requested time frame. Select the correct appointment type and book it with the patient's agreement.

Confirm clearly: 'Your follow-up is scheduled for Thursday, June 10th at 2 PM with Dr. Martinez.' Give them an appointment card and make sure reminders are set. If there are any prep instructions—like fasting for labs—tell them now."

---

### **\[1:40-2:20\] WHEN PATIENTS DECLINE**

**VISUAL:** Conversation illustration showing compassionate interaction, with documentation note.

**DISPLAY TEXT:**

When a Patient Declines to Schedule

Don't just let it go. Instead:

1\. Explain importance of follow-up care  
   "The provider wants to monitor your progress..."

2\. Offer an alternative  
   "Would you like me to call you in \[X\] weeks  
   to schedule then?"

3\. Document the declination in the chart

4\. Place on recall list for future outreach

5\. Provide clinic contact info for self-scheduling

**NARRATOR:** "Sometimes patients don't want to schedule. They're busy, they feel fine, they'll call later. Don't just let it go.

Gently explain why the follow-up matters: 'The provider wants to monitor your progress and make sure the medication is working.'

Offer an alternative: 'Would you like me to call you in a few weeks to schedule then?'

Document that they declined. Add them to the recall list so someone follows up later. And give them the clinic number so they can call when they're ready."

---

### **\[2:20-3:10\] RECALL MANAGEMENT**

**VISUAL:** Recall list report showing patient names, care needed, and outreach status.

**DISPLAY TEXT:**

RECALL MANAGEMENT

Recalls \= Patients overdue for routine care

Examples:  
• Annual physicals  
• Chronic disease follow-ups (diabetes, hypertension)  
• Preventive screenings  
• Immunizations due

Weekly Tasks:  
• Review recall list  
• Contact overdue patients  
• Use phone, portal message, or mail  
• Document ALL outreach attempts  
• Update status when scheduled

Follow clinic policy: typically 3 contact attempts

**NARRATOR:** "Recalls are different from follow-ups. These are patients who are overdue for routine care—annual physicals, chronic disease check-ins, preventive screenings, immunizations.

Review the recall list weekly. Contact overdue patients by phone, portal message, or mail. Document every outreach attempt, even if you just left a voicemail.

Most clinics have a policy—typically three contact attempts before closing the outreach cycle. When someone schedules, update their recall status immediately."

---

### **\[3:10-3:40\] DOCUMENTATION MATTERS**

**VISUAL:** Documentation screen showing outreach log with dates, methods, and outcomes.

**DISPLAY TEXT:**

Document Every Attempt

For each outreach, record:  
• Date and time of contact  
• Method used (phone, portal, letter)  
• Outcome:  
  \- Scheduled (note appointment date)  
  \- Declined (note reason if given)  
  \- Left voicemail  
  \- No answer  
  \- Wrong number/disconnected  
• Any follow-up needed

If it's not documented, it didn't happen.

**NARRATOR:** "Documentation isn't optional. Record the date, the method you used, and the outcome. Did they schedule? Decline? Did you leave a voicemail? Was the number disconnected?

This documentation matters for quality reporting, for continuity of care, and for the next person who pulls this recall list.

If it's not documented, it didn't happen."

---

### **\[3:40-4:00\] CLOSING \- Preventing Care Gaps**

**VISUAL:** Simple visual showing patient pathway with follow-up connecting visits.

**DISPLAY TEXT:**

Follow-Ups & Recalls

You are the connection between:  
Visit → Follow-up → Ongoing Care

• Read provider instructions carefully  
• Schedule within the requested time frame  
• Don't let declines disappear—document & recall  
• Track outreach attempts

Patients don't fall through the cracks  
when you close the loop.

**NARRATOR:** "Follow-ups and recalls are how you close the loop on patient care. Read instructions carefully. Schedule within the time frame. Don't let declines disappear. Track your outreach.

When you do this well, patients don't fall through the cracks. That's continuity of care—and it starts at the front desk."

# **LESSON 4: Appointment Reminder Calls**

**Duration:** 4 minutes **Learning Objective:** Make effective reminder calls that reduce no-shows, handle rescheduling requests, and document outcomes properly. **Workflow Reference:** WF \#7

---

## **SCRIPT**

### **\[0:00-0:25\] OPENING \- Preventing No-Shows**

**VISUAL:** Calendar showing appointments with some marked as "No-Show" crossed out, contrasted with confirmed appointments.

**DISPLAY TEXT:**

Appointment Reminder Calls

Preventing No-Shows Before They Happen

**NARRATOR:** "No-shows waste provider time, delay care for other patients, and often mean the patient who missed gets sicker before they're seen.

Reminder calls are your best tool for preventing no-shows. A quick call 48 to 72 hours before the appointment gives patients time to confirm—or reschedule if they can't make it.

Here's how to make these calls efficient and effective."

---

### **\[0:25-0:55\] STEP 1 \- Generate Your List**

**VISUAL:** Report screen showing appointment list filtered for 2-3 days out.

**DISPLAY TEXT:**

STEP 1: Generate Reminder List

Pull appointments for 2-3 days out

Prioritize:  
• New patients (highest no-show risk)  
• Procedures or longer appointments  
• Patients with history of no-shows  
• Appointments with special prep required

Note any special instructions to relay

**NARRATOR:** "Each day, pull the appointment list for two to three days ahead. Prioritize who you call first.

New patients are highest risk—they've never been to your clinic and might not show. Procedures and longer appointments are harder to fill if someone cancels last-minute. Patients with a history of no-shows need that extra reminder.

Note any special instructions you need to relay—fasting requirements, what to bring, early arrival times."

---

### **\[0:55-1:45\] STEP 2 \- The Reminder Call Script**

**VISUAL:** Phone with script displayed, showing professional but warm approach.

**DISPLAY TEXT:**

STEP 2: Make the Call

Standard Script:

"Hello, this is \[Your Name\] calling from   
\[Clinic Name\] to remind you of your   
appointment on \[Day\], \[Date\] at \[Time\]   
with \[Provider\].

Please arrive 10-15 minutes early.

If you need to reschedule, please call us   
at \[Phone Number\].

Thank you\!"

If no answer: Leave voicemail using same script

**NARRATOR:** "Keep your calls professional but friendly. Here's a standard script:

'Hello, this is Sarah calling from Riverside Family Medicine to remind you of your appointment on Thursday, March 10th at 2:30 PM with Dr. Patel. Please arrive 10 to 15 minutes early. If you need to reschedule, please call us at 555-1234. Thank you\!'

If you get voicemail, leave the same message. Speak clearly, especially when stating the date, time, and callback number."

---

### **\[1:45-2:25\] STEP 3 \- Handle Rescheduling**

**VISUAL:** Split screen showing phone conversation and scheduling calendar.

**DISPLAY TEXT:**

STEP 3: Handle Rescheduling Requests

When patient needs to reschedule:

1\. Handle it NOW—don't ask them to call back

2\. Offer 2-3 alternative times  
   "I have Tuesday at 10 AM or Thursday at 3 PM"

3\. Update the system immediately  
   • Cancel original appointment  
   • Book new appointment  
   • Update reminders

4\. Confirm new appointment before ending call

5\. Send new confirmation if applicable

**NARRATOR:** "When someone says they can't make their appointment, handle the reschedule right then. Don't ask them to call back—they might not.

Offer two or three alternative times: 'I have Tuesday at 10 AM or Thursday at 3 PM—which works better for you?'

Update the system immediately. Cancel the original, book the new appointment, and make sure reminders are updated. Confirm the new time before you hang up.

The goal is to keep them scheduled, not just mark the original as cancelled."

---

### **\[2:25-3:05\] STEP 4 \- Document Outcomes**

**VISUAL:** Documentation screen showing outcome options.

**DISPLAY TEXT:**

STEP 4: Document Every Call

Record the outcome:

• Confirmed \- Patient acknowledged appointment  
• Left voicemail \- Message left, no live contact  
• Rescheduled \- Note new date and time  
• Cancelled \- Note reason if given  
• Unable to reach \- Wrong number, disconnected  
• Declined reminder \- Patient asked not to be called

Track patterns:  
Patients who never answer may have  
outdated contact info or prefer text/email

**NARRATOR:** "Document the outcome of every call. Confirmed means you spoke to them and they acknowledged the appointment. Left voicemail means you left a message but didn't reach them directly. Note rescheduled appointments with the new date. For cancellations, note the reason if they gave one.

If you can't reach someone—wrong number, disconnected—flag that for follow-up. Their contact information may need updating. Some patients consistently don't answer calls—they might prefer text or email reminders instead."

---

### **\[3:05-3:40\] SPECIAL SITUATIONS**

**VISUAL:** Tips callout box with special scenarios.

**DISPLAY TEXT:**

Special Situations

Appointments with prep instructions:  
• Emphasize fasting, medication holds, etc.  
• "Remember, nothing to eat or drink   
  after midnight"

New patients:  
• Give directions and parking info  
• Remind them to arrive early for paperwork  
• Confirm what to bring

Procedures:  
• Verify they have a driver if required  
• Review any pre-procedure instructions

**NARRATOR:** "Some calls need extra information.

For appointments with prep—like fasting labs or procedures—emphasize the instructions: 'Remember, nothing to eat or drink after midnight.'

For new patients, give directions and parking info. Remind them to arrive early for paperwork and confirm what to bring.

For procedures, verify they have a driver if sedation is involved. Review any pre-procedure instructions.

These extra details prevent day-of problems."

---

### **\[3:40-4:00\] CLOSING \- A Few Minutes That Matter**
