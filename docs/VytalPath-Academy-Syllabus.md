# Section 3: Insurance & Billing

**Estimated Time:** 120 minutes + interactive exercises
**Format:** Video lessons + reading modules + quizzes + interactive explorers
**Knowledge Domains:** Billing & Revenue Cycle, Patient Encounter

## Learning Objectives

Upon completion, learners will be able to:

- Explain the purpose and structure of health insurance
- Identify major payer types (commercial, Medicare, Medicaid, TRICARE) and plan types (HMO, PPO, EPO)
- Define key insurance terms: premium, deductible, copay, coinsurance, out-of-pocket maximum
- Read and interpret an insurance card
- Perform real-time eligibility verification
- Calculate patient payment responsibility (copays, deductibles, coinsurance)
- Explain government insurance programs and their eligibility requirements
- Apply coordination of benefits rules including the birthday rule
- Read and explain an Explanation of Benefits (EOB)
- Describe the claim submission process including ERAs and clearinghouses
- Present an Advanced Beneficiary Notice (ABN) to Medicare patients
- Trace a patient encounter through all seven phases of the revenue cycle
- Compare healthcare payment models (fee-for-service, capitation, value-based, bundled)
- Describe financial assistance programs, aging reports, and the collections process

How to calculate patient coinsurance responsibility and explain it to patients. Includes worked examples and common coinsurance splits (80/20, 70/30, 60/40).

Best practices for collecting copays, coinsurance, and outstanding balances at the front desk. Covers scripting, receipt procedures, and handling patients who cannot pay.

# Section 6: Medical Terminology

**Estimated Time:** 50 minutes + unlimited flashcard practice
**Format:** Video lessons + reading modules + quiz + interactive flashcards + term builder
**Knowledge Domains:** Foundational Knowledge

## Learning Objectives

Upon completion, learners will be able to:

- Decode medical terms by breaking them into prefix, root, and suffix components
- Define common medical prefixes for size, position, quantity, and negation
- Identify root words for major body systems, organs, and anatomical structures
- Recognize suffixes indicating conditions, procedures, and specialties
- Use standard medical abbreviations correctly in clinical communication
- Identify and avoid abbreviations on the Joint Commission's "Do Not Use" list
- Navigate body systems using anatomical directional terms
- Recognize common symptoms and conditions discussed at the front desk

---

### Module: Medical Terminology

| # | Lesson | Format | Duration |
|---|--------|--------|----------|
| 1 | Common Abbreviations in Healthcare | Video | 5 min |
| 2 | Word Building: Decoding Medical Terms | Video | 5 min |
| 3 | Common Prefixes | Reading | 10 min |
| 4 | Common Root Words | Reading | 12 min |
| 5 | Common Suffixes | Reading | 10 min |
| 6 | Common Medical Abbreviations | Reading | 10 min |
| 7 | The "Do Not Use" List | Reading | 12 min |
| 8 | Body Systems: Your Navigation Guide | Reading | 15 min |
| 9 | What You'll Hear at the Front Desk | Reading | 15 min |

**1. Common Abbreviations in Healthcare**
Master the abbreviations you will encounter daily in clinical settings — from vital signs (BP, HR, RR) to visit types (NP, EST, AWV) to departments (ED, OR, ICU).

**2. Word Building: Decoding Medical Terms**
Learn the systematic approach to breaking down and understanding medical terms using prefixes, roots, and suffixes. Practice with common clinical terms.

**3. Common Prefixes**
Master essential prefixes for size (macro-, micro-), position (sub-, supra-), quantity (uni-, bi-, poly-), negatives (a-, an-), and colors (cyan-, erythr-) used in medical terminology.

**4. Common Root Words**
Learn the root words for body systems (cardi/o, pulmon/o, neur/o), organs (hepat/o, nephr/o, gastr/o), and anatomical structures. Build vocabulary for every major body system.

**5. Common Suffixes**
Understand suffixes that indicate conditions (-itis, -osis, -emia), procedures (-ectomy, -otomy, -oscopy), and medical specialties (-ology, -ologist, -iatrics).

**6. Common Medical Abbreviations**
Master abbreviations for timing (BID, TID, PRN), routes (PO, IV, IM), diagnoses (DM, HTN, COPD), and tests (CBC, BMP, UA) used daily in clinic settings.

**7. The "Do Not Use" List**
Learn the dangerous abbreviations banned by the Joint Commission — and why they can cause patient harm. Covers U, IU, QD, QOD, trailing zeros, and their required alternatives.

**8. Body Systems: Your Navigation Guide**
Master anatomical directions (superior, inferior, medial, lateral) and body system roots to decode medical terms and route information correctly within the clinic.

**9. What You'll Hear at the Front Desk**
Recognize common symptoms and conditions by body system — chest pain, SOB, N/V, HA, URI — and know when to escalate immediately vs. take a message.

**Assessment:** Medical Terminology Quiz — 80% passing score, 3 attempts
